# Alert Response Guide

Operational detail that does not fit in SKILL.md. Every instruction here is taken
from Pikud Ha'Oref's own guidance; the article path is given for each block so it
can be re-checked. Article content is served at
`https://api.oref.org.il/api/v1/articles//heb/articles/<path>`.

## Telling the alerts apart

Source: `life-saving-guidelines/means-of-alert/1109`, `security-threats/main/2000`.

Pikud Ha'Oref alerts reach the public through the **app**, the **national
emergency portal** (which alerts by sound and display on a home computer only
while the site is open in a browser), and the **public sirens**.

The sound tells you which threat you are dealing with:

| What you hear | Threat | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| A rising-and-falling siren, or the Tzeva Adom alert | Rocket or missile fire | Enter the protected space within your protection time |
| A short siren burst followed by spoken text naming the threat | Anything else | The spoken text states the threat and the required action. Read or listen to it; the action is not always "enter the protected space" |
| A short burst then the announcement "חדירת כלי טיס עוין" | Hostile aircraft or drone | Enter the best protected space you have immediately, and stay there unless a further alert or instruction arrives |

**The alert text is the instruction.** The app states the threat type and what to
do, in the language configured on the device (Hebrew, Arabic, English, Russian).
Do not assume every alert means the rocket protocol.

For deaf and hard-of-hearing users the app can deliver a 10-second vibration, and
on Android a torch flash alongside it. On Android this is configured in the app's
settings page; on iPhone the vibration is configured in the device settings.

## Do not run

Source: `info/preparing-protected-space/1204`.

Running to the protected space causes falls and injuries, and in most of the
country the protection time is enough to walk. Choose the protected space in
advance, plan the route, and clear it of obstacles and objects that could cause a
fall. Pikud Ha'Oref's phrasing is that there is no point panicking: the time
available allows a deep breath and a calm, safe arrival.

For families:

- Make sure the children already know where the protected space is, and practise
  entering it so everyone knows what to do.
- Prepare the route together. Painted footstep markers to the protected space
  work well with young children.
- **Where the protection time is short, have the children sleep inside the
  protected space.** Less comfortable, more safe.
- Children do not have a full grasp of time. Explain that the protection time is
  the time available to get in, not a countdown to an impact.
- When away from home, agree a "what if" plan naming the most protected place
  wherever you are.

## Ventilation between alerts

Source: `info/preparing-protected-space/1204`, `info/preparing-protected-space/1215`.

Pikud Ha'Oref's own instruction for a protected space being slept in is that the
**ventilation openings, the door and the window, stay OPEN and are closed only
when an alert is received**. A mamad is sealed for the alert, not for the night.

Where an NBC ventilation and filtration system is fitted, see article 1215 for
what the system contains, when it is recommended to use it and how to operate it.
Most mamadim in Israel have only the connection point and no system, so do not
tell a user to "switch the system to ventilation mode" without first establishing
that they have one.

**Life-safety override.** The rule that you stay until an explicit instruction
governs the missile threat. It is not a reason to remain in a space that has
itself become dangerous, for example fire, smoke ingress, a gas smell or
structural damage. Leave, get clear, and call the relevant emergency service.

## An alert during a crowded event or in a public venue

Source: `life-saving-guidelines/lsg/1401`.

Noise makes the alert hard to hear and a crowd rushing at once for the protected
space creates crush and trampling injuries. The guidance covers shopping centres,
entertainment shows, weddings, synagogues and cultural events.

- **The organisers must stop the event immediately on the alert and tell the
  audience.**
- If it is possible to lie down, everyone lies on the ground and protects the
  head with the hands, next to a wall or cover if there is one.
- If lying down is not possible, crouch as low as possible to reduce the body's
  silhouette, and protect the head with the hands, next to a wall or cover.
- If the event is a seated one indoors, **stay seated** and protect the head with
  the hands.

The closer to the ground, the lower the risk of injury from fragments. The same
logic applies to anyone caught in a crowded space they do not know: follow the
venue's staff and public-address system rather than running for the street.

## Unexploded ordnance and interceptor debris after an impact

Source: `info/iron-swords/1102`.

Some munitions used against Israel disperse smaller submunitions over a wide
area, and after such an impact part of them can remain on the ground unexploded.
**These can detonate on contact, and being near them endangers life.**

The rule is three words: move away, move others away, report.

- Move away from the area, keep the curious away from it, and report any
  suspicious object to the security forces immediately.
- **Do not photograph** unexploded munitions, fallen ordnance or unidentified
  objects, do not stand near them, and do not touch them in any way.
- Before children go out to a balcony, a yard, a playground or a sports pitch,
  scan the area for suspicious objects, and explain to the children what to do if
  they find one.

## How the release message arrives

Source: `info/iron-swords/1102`, FAQ 23626.

Pikud Ha'Oref sends the message to leave the protected space at the end of an
emergency event through: the Pikud Ha'Oref app; the national emergency portal;
the Cell Broadcast "personal message" system, using the device's ordinary
notification sound; the Pikud Ha'Oref Telegram channels; and the regional radio
stations. You can also call 104 and listen to the current instructions.

## Broadcast fallback: radio and television

Source: `about/main/1003`, `info/iron-swords/1102` FAQ 23626.

If mobile data is gone, broadcast is how the preliminary advisory, the alerts and
the **release message** reach you. Pikud Ha'Oref directs the public to the
relevant regional station rather than a single national frequency, and names:

| Station | Frequencies (FM) |
|---|---|
| Kol BaRama | 105.7, 104.3, 92.1, 107.6 |
| Kol Chai | 92.8, 93, 102.5 |
| Radio Darom | 101.5 |
| Galei Yisrael | 106.5, 94, 89.3 |
| Kan Moreshet (weekends and festivals only) | 90.5, 90.8, 92.5, 100.7 |

Television: channels **11, 12, 13, 14, 9 and 140**.

Pikud Ha'Oref does NOT name Galei Zahal 102.3 or Reshet Bet 95.5. Older guidance
did; do not repeat it.

Sabbath-observant users: stations that run a silent carrier through Shabbat and
festivals carry the alerts without requiring the set to be operated.

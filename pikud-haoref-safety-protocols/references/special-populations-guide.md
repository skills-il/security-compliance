# Special Populations Emergency Guide

## Children by Age Group

### Infants (0-1 year)
- Carry to shelter immediately
- Keep baby carrier/car seat in or near the mamad
- Bring bottles, formula, diapers
- The baby's crib should NOT be in front of the mamad window

### Toddlers (1-3 years)
- Carry or guide to shelter by hand
- Use a calm, reassuring voice
- Practice makes it routine: "We hear the siren, we go to our room"
- Keep a favorite toy/blanket permanently in the mamad

### Young Children (3-6 years)
- Can walk to shelter independently if practiced
- Use simple explanations: "The siren tells us to go to our safe room until it's quiet"
- Avoid adult news and graphic content
- Drawing and play after the event helps process emotions

### School-Age (6-12 years)
- Understand the purpose of shelters
- Can help younger siblings
- May have anxiety: acknowledge their feelings
- Assign age-appropriate tasks ("you're in charge of the flashlight")

### Teenagers (13-18 years)
- May minimize danger or resist going to shelter
- Include them in family emergency planning
- They can help monitor the Home Front Command app
- Watch for signs of stress they may not verbalize

## Elderly Residents

### Preparation
- Pre-arrange assistance with neighbors or building committee (vaad bayit)
- Keep a "go bag" next to the bed: medications, glasses, hearing aid, phone, flashlight
- If using a wheelchair or walker, ensure the path to shelter is clear and wide enough
- Register with Home Front Command (104/1207) for special needs support

### During an Alert
- If you cannot reach the shelter in time, use the nearest interior room
- Sit against an interior wall, away from windows
- If you fall, stay on the ground and protect your head
- After the alert, wait for assistance if needed before moving

### Post-Alert
- Check for injuries
- Take medications on schedule even if routine is disrupted
- Contact family or neighbors to confirm safety
- If your residence is damaged, call the municipality (*106)

## People with Physical Disabilities

### Wheelchair Users
- Practice the route to shelter and measure doorway widths
- If the mamad door is too narrow, designate the safest accessible interior space
- Inform the building committee so they can assist during alerts
- Lock wheelchair brakes when positioned in the shelter

### Mobility Limitations (walker, crutches)
- Keep assistive device next to you at all times, especially at night
- Consider a ground-floor apartment if possible
- The stairwell protocol (for buildings without mamad) may not be feasible: use interior room instead

## People with Sensory Disabilities

### Hearing Impaired
- Install the Home Front Command app with vibration and visual alerts enabled
- Use a vibrating alerter device for nighttime (bed shaker)
- Establish visual signals with household members
- Flashing light systems can be installed connected to the siren system

### Visually Impaired
- Walk the route to shelter daily until it becomes automatic
- Keep the path COMPLETELY clear of obstacles (shoes, toys, furniture)
- Use tactile markers (raised tape on floor) to guide the route
- Practice closing the mamad door by feel
- Keep a cane or guide dog harness near the bed

## People with Cognitive/Developmental Disabilities
- Use visual schedules showing the shelter routine step by step
- Practice frequently with positive reinforcement
- Consider sensory needs: noise-canceling headphones for siren sensitivity
- Assign a specific caregiver for emergency escort
- Carry an identification card with emergency contacts and medical info

## People with PTSD or Anxiety Disorders
- The siren can be a severe trigger
- Pre-position anti-anxiety medications in the shelter
- Use grounding techniques: 5-4-3-2-1 (5 things you see, 4 you hear, etc.)
- Noise-canceling headphones can reduce siren anxiety
- After the alert, it is normal to feel heightened anxiety. Contact ERAN (1201) for emotional support hotline
- Consider therapy focused on emergency preparedness to reduce anticipatory anxiety

## New Immigrants (Olim)

### First Steps After Arrival
1. Learn your time-to-shelter zone (oref.org.il or call 104)
2. Download the Home Front Command app (available in Hebrew, English, Russian, Arabic, Amharic, French)
3. Identify your building's shelter (ask neighbors, landlord, or building committee)
4. Practice the route from every room in your apartment
5. Prepare supplies in the shelter

### Language Resources
- Home Front Command hotline in English: 104
- Nefesh B'Nefesh emergency guide: nbn.org.il/emergency
- AACI emergency handbook: aaci.org.il
- Many municipalities have immigrant liaison officers who can help in multiple languages

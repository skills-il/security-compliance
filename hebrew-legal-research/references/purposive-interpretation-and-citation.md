# Purposive interpretation, citation format and doctrinal traps

Supplement to `SKILL.md`. Read it when the user needs a statutory interpretation, a citation check, or an analysis that touches international law. Everything here is a working aid: check each authority against an official source before citing it, and follow the legal notice in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Four steps of purposive interpretation (Israeli method)

For any statutory analysis, walk through these steps in order:

1. **Text (the range of linguistic meanings).** The words are the starting point and the outer limit. List the reasonable linguistic meanings of the Hebrew text. Do not go beyond them.
2. **Subjective purpose (the legislator's intent).** Draw the aims the actual legislator meant to achieve from the Knesset record, the explanatory notes to the bill (דברי הסבר), and the social and historical context at the time of enactment.
3. **Objective purpose (the system's basic values).** The values a democratic legal system wants to advance: human rights, justice, equality, fairness, reasonableness, legal certainty and coherence of legislation.
4. **Weighing and decision.** Choose, among the linguistically possible meanings, the one that best serves the purpose of the law. When the subjective and objective purposes conflict, decide by the strength of the sources, the branch of law (constitutional versus ordinary legislation), and the age of the statute. State the reasoning.

## 2. Two-track research for complex questions

For multi-branch questions, run two tracks and then merge them:

- **Track 1, legislation:** the primary and secondary provisions (statutes, ordinances, regulations, orders), the legislative history, the purpose, the current in-force status and the commencement date of recent amendments.
- **Track 2, case law:** the binding Supreme Court rulings, separating the ratio from obiter dicta and majority from minority opinions, and checking that each ruling is still good law (not distinguished or overruled).
- **Synthesis:** combine both into one coherent analysis, apply the tests to the facts, flag doctrinal traps, and never invent a precedent. A citation that was not verified is marked "needs verification".

## 4. Doctrinal traps in analysis

1. **"Decides" versus "recommends".** UN Security Council decisions under Chapter VII are binding. UN General Assembly resolutions are declaratory or recommendatory, unless they reflect a customary rule that has crystallized.
2. **Ex ante, not ex post.** Judge the reasonableness of a decision (governmental, business or military) at the time it was made, not with hindsight.
3. **Jus in bello is independent of jus ad bellum.** The laws of armed conflict apply equally to both sides, whoever started the aggression.
4. **Effective control versus overall control.** Two attribution tests for state responsibility: effective control (ICJ, *Nicaragua*, 1986) and overall control (ICTY Appeals Chamber, *Tadić*, 1999).
5. **Persistent objector.** A state can avoid being bound by a customary rule only through explicit, consistent and continuous objection from the time the custom was forming (ICJ, *Fisheries*, 1951).
6. **No invented authorities.** Do not cite a ruling that was not confirmed in an official database (Nevo, Takdin, the Judicial Authority site, the Knesset site). A doubtful authority is flagged clearly as needing verification.
